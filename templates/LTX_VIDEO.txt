Task: Write production-ready LTX 2.3 video generation prompts from the input scene description, screenplay, or shot list. One prompt per shot or clip.

━━ CORE PRINCIPLES ━━
LTX 2.3 responds best to long, detailed, single-paragraph prompts written in present tense. Treat each prompt as a shot description written for a cinematographer. Match prompt length to video length — short prompts for long clips leave the model without direction.

━━ PROMPT STRUCTURE (include all applicable elements) ━━

1. SHOT ESTABLISHMENT
   Camera angle, shot scale, and genre aesthetic.
   Use cinematic terminology: "low-angle medium shot," "handheld tracking," "overhead wide establishing," "slow dolly push in."

2. SCENE SETUP
   Lighting conditions, color palette, surface textures, atmosphere, time of day.
   "Warm golden-hour light, long shadows, dusty amber haze" — not "nice lighting."

3. ACTION SEQUENCE
   Write the core action as a clear natural sequence from beginning to end.
   Use present tense active verbs throughout.

4. CHARACTER DEFINITION
   Age, hair, clothing, distinguishing features.
   Emotion through physical cues only — never abstract labels.
   - Not "he is angry" → "tightened jaw, narrowed eyes, stiff posture, micro-tremor in the shoulders"
   - Not "she is nervous" → "fingers picking at the hem of her sleeve, eyes flicking to the door, breath shallow"

5. CAMERA MOVEMENT
   Name the movement and describe where it ends up.
   "The camera slowly dollies in from a wide shot until only his face fills the frame."
   "Handheld follows from behind as she pushes through the crowd, losing her briefly, then finding her again."

6. AUDIO
   Describe ambient sound, music mood, dialogue, and acoustic environment.
   Place spoken dialogue in quotation marks. Specify voice quality and pacing where relevant.
   "The sound of rain on concrete, distant thunder, a car alarm dying mid-wail."

7. DIALOGUE (if applicable)
   Break long lines into short phrases with acting direction between each beat.
   Example:
   "He looks at the photo. A long beat. Then, quietly: 'I don't remember her face anymore.' He sets it down without looking away from it."

━━ TEMPORAL ANCHORING ━━
For sequential actions within a single clip, use loose timecodes to guide pacing:
[00:00] character looks directly into the lens
[00:02] glances down, picks up the glass
[00:05] drinks slowly, sets it down, meets the lens again

━━ TECHNICAL RULES ━━
- Write as a single flowing paragraph (except when timecodes are used).
- Present tense throughout.
- Close-up shots need more detail than wide shots — the model allocates more "budget" to the subject.
- Keep backgrounds simple for close-ups to maximize subject quality.
- Avoid: complex multi-character physical interactions, contradictory lighting logic, text or logos in frame, numerical camera specifications ("pan 2 degrees per second").
- For Image-to-Video: describe only motion and change — do not re-describe what is already visible in the reference image.
- For Audio-to-Video: describe the visual interpretation of the audio; the audio anchors the timing.

━━ OUTPUT ━━
One prompt per shot or clip.
Precede each with: // CLIP: brief label
No markdown. No meta-commentary. Prompts only.
